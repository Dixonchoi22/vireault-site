How Vireault encrypts your files

Short answer: Vireault encrypts vault contents on your Android phone with AES-256-GCM. A random 256-bit master key does the encryption. Your PIN, 3×3 or 5×5 pattern or passphrase unlocks that key through Argon2id. We run no vault servers and hold no keys, so we cannot perform a remote reset. If you keep a user-held 12-word recovery phrase, you can use it locally to choose a new unlock secret. This page states what ships today and what encryption does not protect against.

Design goals

  • On-device only. Vault bytes stay in app-private storage unless you export a backup file you control.
  • No account. There is no Vireault login, cloud sync or remote recovery.
  • Authenticated encryption. AES-256-GCM both encrypts and detects tampering of vault blobs.
  • Honest limits. We describe weak spots instead of marketing “military-grade” with no detail.

What happens when you unlock

  1. You enter a PIN, draw a 3×3 or 5×5 pattern, or type a passphrase.
  2. Argon2id stretches that secret with a per-vault salt (default about 64 MiB of memory on phones that can spare it; 32 MiB on tighter devices).
  3. The derived key unwraps a random master key stored in the vault header (AES-256-GCM wrap).
  4. That master key decrypts the encrypted index and file blobs while the vault stays open.
  5. When the vault locks, the in-app master key material the app controls is wiped so further reads fail closed.

New vaults use Argon2id. Older vaults created under a legacy PBKDF2 header format are upgraded on a successful unlock. Details live in the openSECURITY.mdin the app repository.

What is encrypted on disk

  • File contents — AES-256-GCM. Newer items use segmented STREAM-style blobs bound to the item id so truncation, reordering or swapping blobs fails authentication.
  • Index / names — the list of items (names, types, sizes) is encrypted, so cleartext titles are not sitting beside the blobs.
  • Thumbnails — encrypted and bound to their item when present.
  • Backups — an exported backup is its own encrypted file, protected by a generated recovery code (not your everyday PIN), with authenticated segments so tampering is detected.

Day-to-day protections

  • Auto-lock when the app goes to the background, and after about five minutes idle. A very fast app switch can race the lock, so we do not claim it is instantaneous.
  • Screenshot / screen-recording blocking on vault screens (FLAG_SECURE). This blocks ordinary app screenshots; a compromised operating system or emulator display capture can still see pixels.
  • Escalating on-device lockout after repeated wrong unlocks.
  • Viewing media and PDFs without writing decrypted temp files to storage.
  • Optional delete of gallery originals after a successful import (Android’s normal delete; cloud copies are yours to remove).

Fingerprint unlock

On supported devices, you can enable fingerprint unlock after unlocking the vault with your secret. Android's biometric prompt controls access through the device Keystore. After a device reboot, the first unlock requires your PIN, pattern or passphrase. Recovery-based changes disable existing biometric unlock. Fingerprint is unavailable in hidden-list mode to avoid revealing the matching vault.

Your 12-word recovery phrase

Setup offers a generated, user-held 12-word phrase. Keep it offline and private: anyone with it can open the vault. It permits choosing a new unlock secret if you forget yours. Skipping requires two explicit confirmations. Without the phrase or a usable separate encrypted backup and its recovery material, a forgotten unlock secret means we cannot recover the vault. There is no email or server reset.

Camera capture and temporary video storage

Photos taken inside the app are saved to encrypted vault storage, not the gallery. Android's video recording pipeline currently requires a seekable temporary file, so plaintext exists in app-private no_backup/capture only while recording normally. If the app goes to the background or the vault locks, the unfinished video is discarded and the temporary file deleted. Emulator testing confirmed deletion on normal backgrounding; abrupt power loss, force-kill and flash remnants were not tested. This is not a claim of guaranteed physical erasure.

What Vireault does not protect against

Encryption is not magic. These limits are intentional or inherent — please read them before trusting irreplaceable media to any vault.

  • Lost secrets. We cannot perform a remote reset. A user-held recovery phrase can reset the unlock secret locally. If you skip that phrase and also lose backup recovery material, nobody can reconstruct the files — including us. There is no email reset of vault contents.
  • Short PINs or patterns against offline copies. On-device lockout does not apply if someone with root or forensic access copies vault files off the phone. A 6-digit PIN has one million combinations; Argon2id makes each guess expensive but cannot enlarge the search space. Prefer a long passphrase for stronger protection.
  • A compromised or rooted OS while the vault is unlocked. Malware with accessibility rights, a malicious screen overlay, or full device compromise is out of scope.
  • Existence leakage. In normal mode, app-private directory names can reveal vault names. Hidden mode removes the normal lock-screen vault list and the secret selects a matching vault; it does not hide the installed app or make storage forensics impossible. Directory counts, approximate sizes and markers can still reveal vault existence. Neither hidden nor decoy use is forensic-proof deniability.
  • Guaranteed flash erasure. Secure delete overwrites then unlinks; flash wear levelling can still leave unreachable remnants. Remnants of vault data remain ciphertext.
  • Files you leave outside the vault. Gallery, messaging and cloud backups can keep copies after import unless you remove them yourself.
  • No cloud sync, and no third-party audit claim. The current build implements folders, notes, 5×5 patterns, compatible-device fingerprint unlock, recovery phrases, camera capture and Pro hidden/decoy and alternate-icon features. We do not claim an independent third-party audit.

Internal emulator checks — 7 November 2026

We tested a debug build on Android 14, not a release-signed app or real hardware. Bounded plaintext scans found no test note title/body or test secrets in app-private files; normal screenshot blocking, hidden-list selection, pattern/recovery flows and biometric unlock were exercised. The review found a camera permission-request crash, caused by an old AndroidX Fragment version rejecting large Activity Result request codes, and a change-secret check that could confirm another vault's secret without normal unlock throttling. Both were fixed in the development tree: Fragment 1.8.5 is pinned, colliding change-secret attempts now use a generic error and count toward the global lockout. Capture of photos stays out of the gallery; unfinished video spools are discarded when the app backgrounds. A very quick Home-and-return once retained the unlocked view, while a confirmed background transition locked it. These checks are not a passed independent audit.

How this differs from “hide” apps

Many Android “photo vault” products primarily hide or rename files, show ads, or rely on an account and vendor cloud. Hiding is not the same as authenticated encryption.
