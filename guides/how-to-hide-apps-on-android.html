How to hide apps on Android

Short answer: On Android 15 or later, use Private Space: it puts apps behind a separate lock and can hide itself too. On a Samsung phone, use Secure Folder for the same job, or the Home screen's Hide apps setting if you only want the icon gone. Removing an icon doesn't lock anything, so choose based on who you're hiding the app from.

Option 1: Private Space (Android 15 and later)

Private Space is a separate area of your phone for apps, built into Android. It's the strongest built-in option if your phone supports it.

  1. Open Settings, then Security & privacy, then Private space.
  2. Confirm your screen lock and tap Set up.
  3. Choose a separate lock. If you skip this, your normal screen lock opens the space, so anyone who knows it gets in.
  4. Install the apps you want to keep private inside the space.
  5. In the Private Space settings, turn on the option to hide it when it's locked.

While it's locked, its apps are fully stopped, so their notifications won't appear until you unlock it. Three things to know first:

  • A hidden space can still be found. You can search for "Private" or open it from Settings, and Google says its existence can't always be hidden from other apps or from a connected computer.
  • A forgotten Private Space lock can't be recovered. You can only reset the space, which deletes it.
  • It isn't included in device backups, so deleting the space deletes its apps and their on-device data.

Option 2: Samsung Secure Folder

On Galaxy phones, Secure Folder is a separate locked area with its own pattern, PIN or password. Find it in Settings under Security and privacy, then add the apps you want inside it. It can be reset with your Samsung account, which is handy if you forget the lock, but it also means your account can get you back in.

Option 3: Hide the icon in your launcher

On many Samsung phones, go to Settings, then Home screen, then Hide apps on Home and Apps screens, and pick your apps. Other brands offer something similar, and the menu names vary by phone and software version, so search "hide apps" in Settings if you can't find it.

This only tidies your screen. The app still appears in the Settings app list, and it opens normally for anyone who finds it. It's fine for clutter or a curious child, but it isn't privacy.

Common mistakes

  • Using the same PIN as your screen lock, which hands the hidden apps to anyone who knows it.
  • Forgetting about files. Photos and downloads an app saved to shared storage can still show up in your gallery after the app is hidden. To protect those, see how to hide private photos on Android.
  • Signing in to the same Google account inside Private Space. Google recommends a separate account, because a shared one can expose private data outside the space.
  • Installing an unknown "app hider" from outside the Play Store. It's an extra app that sees your activity, and it may do less than the options already on your phone.
