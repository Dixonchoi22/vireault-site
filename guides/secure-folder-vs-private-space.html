Secure Folder vs Private Space: which should you use?

Short answer: use Secure Folder if you have a Samsung phone that supports it and want a lock you can reset with your Samsung account. Use Private Space if you're on Android 15 or later and want apps fully stopped while locked, with no recovery route for anyone. Both are mainly about keeping apps private. If your concern is files you want to carry between phones, neither is built for that.

The details below come from Google's Private Space help page, the Android documentation and Samsung's Secure Folder FAQ, checked in November 2026. Menus and features vary by phone and change over time.

When Secure Folder is the better choice

If you have a supported Samsung phone, it's already there and it's built for files as well as apps. Samsung describes it as a place for photos, videos, files, apps and data, and you can move items in from its own apps, such as Gallery.

The Samsung Account reset is the main trade-off. It saves you if you forget the lock, but it also means your Samsung Account password can get you past that lock, so protect that account well.

When Private Space is the better choice

It's the option if you're not on a Samsung phone. Choose a different lock when you set it up, because if you use your screen lock, anyone who knows that can open it.

It's also the stricter of the two. Google says locked apps can't run in the foreground or background, and they're hidden from the launcher, recents, sharing and Settings. You can hide the space's existence too, though Google warns that doesn't hide it from someone connecting the phone to a computer, from device logs, or from apps that detect it.

The cost is that there's no safety net. Google says that if you forget the lock, you won't be able to access the space, and it won't be restored from a device backup.

What neither one is for

Both keep things on one phone. Private Space's on-device data isn't backed up, and Samsung's FAQ doesn't describe moving a Secure Folder to a different phone. If you want private files that can move with you, you need something built for that.

An encrypted vault such as Vireault does a different job. It doesn't hide apps at all, so it can't replace either of these for private messaging or banking apps. It holds files, works on Android 8 and later, makes an encrypted backup file you can restore on another phone, and has no account. The catch is that a forgotten PIN, pattern or passphrase can't be reset by anyone.

Common mistakes

  • Using your screen lock for Private Space. That hands it to anyone who knows your phone's PIN.
  • Treating Private Space as a backup. Google says its on-device data isn't backed up, so resetting the phone can lose it.
  • Forgetting the Samsung Account password. If you lose both that and your Secure Folder lock, Samsung says to contact its support.
  • Leaving originals behind. Copying a file in leaves the original where it was. Moving it, or deleting the original, is what removes it.

Common questions

Can I use Secure Folder and Private Space on the same phone?

Neither Samsung's FAQ nor Google's help page covers this, so check your own phone's Settings. A Samsung phone on Android 15 or later may show both.

Is Private Space encrypted?

Google's help page and the Android documentation don't describe its encryption, so we won't claim anything beyond them. They describe it as a separate profile that's stopped while locked.

What happens if I forget my Private Space lock?

You can't get into the space. Google says you can only delete it, which removes what's inside.
