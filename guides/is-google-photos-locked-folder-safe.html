Is Google Photos Locked Folder safe?

Short answer: Locked Folder is safe enough for keeping photos away from your main grid, your memories and other apps. It isn't safe from anyone who knows your phone's screen lock, because that's what opens it. Google also doesn't describe it as end-to-end encrypted. For photos you truly need to keep private, you want a separate secret.

The details below come from Google's Locked Folder help page, checked in November 2026. Features change, so check that page for the latest.

What Locked Folder protects you from

  • People scrolling your photos. Google says items won't appear in the Photos grid, memories, search or albums.
  • Other apps. Google says items won't be available to other apps on your device that have access to your photos and videos.
  • Your TV. Google says they won't show on smart displays such as Nest Hub or through Chromecast.
  • Accidental sharing. You have to move an item out of the folder before you can share it.

What it doesn't protect you from

  • Anyone who knows your screen lock. Google is direct about this: "People who have access to the device screen lock can unlock Locked Folder." You can't set a different password, so a partner, a child, or anyone who has watched you unlock your phone can open it.
  • Losing the photos. If backup is off, items are stored only on that device. Google says they're deleted if you factory reset the device, clear the app's data, or delete the Photos app. A lost or damaged phone takes them too.
  • Questions about who can read backups. Backup is optional. Google says Locked Folder is "protected by Google Photos' standard encryption practices", and the page doesn't say it's end-to-end encrypted. If you need a provider to be unable to read your photos, that wording doesn't promise it.

How to use Locked Folder more safely

  1. Use a screen lock that other people don't know, and don't unlock your phone where someone can watch.
  2. Decide on backup deliberately. With it off, your photos exist only on that phone. With it on, they're in your Google Account, so protect that account with a strong password and two-step verification.
  3. After moving photos in, check that older copies aren't left in other backups or apps.
  4. Before you reset or replace your phone, check where your Locked Folder items are, because items that aren't backed up don't survive a reset.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming it has its own password. It doesn't. Your screen lock is the password.
  • Assuming it's backed up. Check the setting. If backup is off, uninstalling the Photos app deletes your items.
  • Treating "encrypted" as "only I can read it". Those aren't the same thing. Ask who holds the key.

Common questions

Can someone else open my Locked Folder?

Yes, if they know your screen lock. It's the only password Locked Folder has.

Does Locked Folder back up to the cloud?

Only if you turn backup on. Items that aren't backed up are stored only on that device.

Is Locked Folder end-to-end encrypted?

Google's help page doesn't say so. It says Locked Folder is protected by Google Photos' standard encryption practices.
