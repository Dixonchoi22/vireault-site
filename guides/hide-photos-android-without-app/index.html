How to hide photos on Android without an app

You can keep many private photos out of the main gallery without installing a third-party vault. The honest catch is that “hidden” is not the same as “encrypted,” and the built-in options differ by Android version and manufacturer. This guide sticks to methods that ship with Google Photos or the system, cites Google’s own docs, and ends with a short checklist for when a local encrypted vault is the better fit.

1. Google Photos Locked Folder

On Android 6 and later, Google Photos can move sensitive items into aLocked Folder. According toGoogle Photos Help, items there do not appear in the Photos grid, Memories, search, or albums, and they are not available to other apps that can read your photos. Unlocking uses yourdevice screen lock — you cannot set a separate Locked Folder password.

Typical steps (wording follows Google’s help article):

  1. Open the Google Photos app.
  2. Tap Collections, then Locked.
  3. Set up Locked Folder if prompted, and authenticate with your screen lock.
  4. Select photos or videos, then choose Add to → Move to Locked Folder.

Google also documents optional backup for Locked Folder. If backup is off, items live only on that device; uninstalling Photos, clearing app data, or losing the phone can wipe them. If backup is on, you can open Locked Folder on other signed-in devices with that device’s screen lock. Anyone who knows the phone lock can open the folder on that phone.

2. Android 15 Private Space (apps and files)

Starting with Android 15, many phones includePrivate space: a separate profile that can hide apps and hold files away from the main user. Google’sAndroid Help pagedescribes setup under Settings → Security & privacy → Private space, a separate lock, and an option to hide the Private space entry when it is locked.

You can move or copy files into Private space with Add → Add files.Move deletes the original from the main profile;Copy leaves the original in place. When Private space is locked, its apps stop, drop out of search and Recents, and leave the photo picker and sharing surfaces.

Limits that matter for photos:

  • Requires Android 15+, supporting hardware, and the main user (not guest or secondary).
  • Google states the space can still be discovered via computer/adb access, device logs, or other apps in some cases — so it is not forensic deniability.
  • On-device Private space data is not part of a normal device backup restore. Deleting the space removes apps and data with no local backup.
  • Google recommends a dedicated Google Account inside Private space so synced photos and history do not leak into the main profile.

Private space is strongest when you install a gallery or vault inside the space and keep the space locked. On its own it isolates files; it does not replace file-level encryption with a secret only you hold.

3. OEM “secure folder” and gallery hide tools

Samsung Secure Folder, Xiaomi Second space, and other manufacturer tools can hide albums or whole workspaces. Steps and strength vary by brand. Treat them like Private space: useful for shared-phone moments, still tied to the vendor account and device lock model. Check your maker’s support pages for the exact menu path on your build.

4. Weak tricks that are not protection

Moving images into an obscure folder, renaming them, or relying on a.nomedia file only changes what some galleries index. A file manager, USB connection, or backup tool can still read the bytes. Do not treat this as privacy for IDs, medical images, or anything you would not leave in Downloads.

5. Checklist: is “without an app” enough?

  1. Who can unlock the phone? Locked Folder and most OEM tools use that same lock.
  2. Did cloud backup already keep a copy outside the hidden place?
  3. Do messaging apps still hold a compressed duplicate?
  4. Can you recover the files if Photos is cleared or Private space is deleted?
  5. Do you need a secret that is not the device PIN (passphrase, separate vault PIN)?

If you need encryption under a vault secret, optional delete of gallery originals after import, and an encrypted backup file you control, that is the job of a vault app — not a renamed folder.
