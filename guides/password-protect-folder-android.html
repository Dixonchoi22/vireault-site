How to password protect a folder on Android

Short answer: Android can't put a password on an ordinary folder. Instead, you move the files into something that has its own lock: Safe folder in Files by Google, Secure Folder on Samsung phones, an encrypted ZIP, or an encrypted vault app. For most people, Safe folder is the quickest free start, and a vault is the better fit if you want a key that's separate from your phone and your accounts.

Option 1: Safe folder in Files by Google

This is free and works on Android 8.0 or later, though the option may not appear on every phone.

  1. Open Files by Google and go to Collections.
  2. Tap Safe folder and set a PIN or pattern.
  3. Find your files, press and hold to select them, then use More to move them to Safe folder.

There's no recovery: if you forget the PIN or pattern, you can't get to your files. Google also warns that someone with unauthorized access to the phone's internal storage may be able to reach them.

Option 2: Samsung Secure Folder

On Galaxy phones, go to Settings, then Security and privacy, then Secure Folder, and pick a pattern, PIN or password. Then move files in from the Secure Folder itself or from My Files. If you forget the lock, you may be able to reset it with your Samsung account, which is convenient but makes that account another way in.

Option 3: Private Space (Android 15 and later)

Private Space locks apps rather than folders, but files saved by apps inside it stay inside it. It suits you if you already use it, and our guide on how to hide apps on Android covers setup.

Option 4: An encrypted ZIP

An archiver such as ZArchiver can create a ZIP protected with AES-256 and a password. It's free, and the file can be moved to any device or cloud drive. The downside is that you have to extract files to open them, and those extracted copies stay readable until you delete them. Use a long password, because a short one is easy to guess once someone has the file.

Option 5: An encrypted vault app

A vault encrypts each file as you add it, and its lock is separate from your phone's screen lock. Check that it really encrypts your files and doesn't just hide them. Our guide on how to encrypt files on Android covers what to look for.

Common mistakes

  • Trusting a "folder lock" app that only hides files. If a file manager or computer can still open them, they aren't encrypted.
  • Leaving the originals behind. Moving a copy into a vault doesn't protect the file you left in Downloads, or the one in your cloud backup.
  • Keeping your only copy in a locked folder. Vireault vaults aren't backed up unless you make an encrypted backup yourself, and a forgotten PIN can't be reset, so keep another copy of anything irreplaceable somewhere safe.
